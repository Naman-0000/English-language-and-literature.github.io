Unit Lexicon

Master these terms to enhance your analysis and writing. Click any row to highlight your current focus.

Word & Type Definition Synonyms Antonyms Usage
Atrophy
(v./n.)		 Gradual decline in effectiveness or vigor due to underuse. Withering, decaying Strengthening, flourishing "After being out of the gym, my muscles began to atrophy."
Bastion
(n.)		 A person or institution strongly defending particular principles; a fortified place. Fortress, stronghold Weakness, vulnerability "With security at every gate, the school has become a bastion of safety."
Concord
(n.)		 Agreement between people or groups. Treaty, harmony Conflict, strife "After many wars, both nations signed a treaty and are now at peace and concord."
Consummate
(adj.)		 Showing a high degree of skill or flair; complete or perfect. Expert, masterly Amateurish, unskilled "The guitarist's performance was a work of consummate skill."
Disarray
(n.)		 A state of organization or untidiness. Disorganization, chaos Order, organization "The student left the house in total disarray when he was late for his exam."
Exigency
(n.)		 An urgent need or demand. Urgency, pressure Ease, comfort "The exigency of water in the town required immediate saving methods."
Flotsam
(n.)		 Floating debris from a ship that has sunk. Wreckage, debris Treasures, assets "After the war, the bastion was a mess of flotsam and wreckage."
Frenetic
(adj.)		 Fast and energetic in a rather wild and uncontrolled way. Frantic, wild Calm, relaxed "The soldiers were frenetic in their attempt to win the war."
Glean
(v.)		 To extract information from various sources bit by bit. Collect, gather Scatter, disperse "It took years for the detective to glean the mystery regarding the case."
Grouse
(v.)		 To complain pettily; grumble. Grumble, whine Compliment, praise "Despite excellent hospitality, there is always one person who likes to grouse."
Incarcerate
(v.)		 To imprison or confine. Jail, lock up Release, liberate "After the conflict, the losing side was incarcerated."
Incumbent
(adj.)		 Necessary for (someone) as a duty or responsibility. Mandatory, required Optional, elective "To enter a top university, high academic standing and extracurriculars are incumbent."
Jocular
(adj.)		 Fond of or characterized by joking; humorous or playful. Witty, humorous Solemn, serious "My English teacher is very helpful, jocular, and supportive."
Ludicrous
(adj.)		 So foolish, unreasonable, or out of place as to be amusing. Absurd, laughable Serious, pathetic "The idea that a toddler can drive a car is ludicrous."
Mordant
(adj.)		 (Especially of humor) having or showing a sharp or critical quality; biting. Sarcastic, acerbic Gentle, mild "The critic was very mordant in his review of the play."
Nettle
(v.)		 To irritate or annoy (someone). Irritate, vex Please, delight "The construction noises began to nettle those working from home."
Pecuniary
(adj.)		 Relating to or consisting of money. Monetary, financial Non-monetary "While the company was large, it offered very low pecuniary rewards."
Pusillanimous
(adj.)		 Showing a lack of courage or determination; timid. Cowardly, fearful Brave, courageous "The soldiers mocked the pusillanimous individual who fled the field."
Recumbent
(adj.)		 (Especially of a person or human figure) lying down. Prone, reclining Upright, standing "During the drill, the soldiers were recumbent to dodge the simulated fire."
Stratagem
(n.)		 A plan or scheme, especially one used to outwit an opponent. Trick, ploy Honesty, frankness "To win the battle, the commander used a stratagem to outmaneuver the enemy."
